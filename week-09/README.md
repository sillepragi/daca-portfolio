# Week 9: Creating a hiring guide (Teamwork)

## Overview
Week 9 focused on moving from data analytics to strategic planning for the hiring process. In light of UrbanStyle’s rapid growth and new investment, our team was tasked with creating a comprehensive hiring guide to find five new, capable data analysts for the company.

This project demonstrates the ability to see the data landscape through the eyes of an employer, defining standards for code quality, consistency, and business value.

## My role: B — Technical interviewer

As a technical interviewer, my responsibilities included assessing candidates’ technical competency and setting criteria for “production-grade” code. My goal was to ensure that new hires were not only able to write queries, but also to navigate UrbanStyle’s data chaos and build sustainable data solutions.

### Key contributions and activities:
* **Evaluation criteria creation:** I developed specific checkpoints for assessing SQL and Python skills. I emphasized that the candidate must master more complex concepts such as **CTEs (Common Table Expressions)** and **window functions** to ensure code readability and efficiency.
* **Understanding syntax:** I assessed the candidate's ability to explain their syntax and queries (e.g., why use a `LEFT JOIN` to find lost customers).
* **Business storytelling:** I assessed the candidates' ability to translate technical results (e.g., RFM score) into understandable business recommendations.

## AI Usage
* **NotebookLM:** I used NotebookLM to align hiring criteria with IT Director expectations for technical accuracy and documentation.

## Key lessons learned
During the week 9 group work, **I learned to look at the hiring process through the employer’s perspective**, taking on the role of a technical interviewer to critically analyze a data analyst’s portfolio and understand what standards of code quality, documentation, and business storytelling are critical to a candidate’s success. I discovered that a strong portfolio of solving real-world business problems is often more telling than a long list of theoretical certifications.
