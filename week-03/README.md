# Week 3: SQL JOINs — Connecting UrbanStyle data

## What I did
- I learned to join different data tables (e.g. `customers`, `sales`, `products`) to find relationships hidden in fragmented data.
- I used different JOIN types according to the task: INNER JOIN to find matching pairs and LEFT JOIN to identify so-called "spirit customers", i.e. registered users who have never made a purchase.
- I joined three or more tables at once to analyze the popularity of product categories in different cities.
- I implemented table aliases (e.g. s, c, p) to make complex queries more readable.
- I participated in a team project where I analyzed the effectiveness of sales channels and prepared a summary report.
- I formalized my work for the week in a GitHub portfolio.

## Key lessons learned
- I learned the difference between Primary Key (a unique identifier in a table) and Foreign Key (a reference to a unique identifier in another table), which is the basis for joining tables.
- I understood how different JOINs filter results – INNER JOIN only returns matches, while LEFT JOIN preserves all information from the left table.
- I understood the importance of translating numbers into business-critical information – for example, finding "lost customers" allows marketing to target them with precisely targeted campaigns (such as a welcome campaign with a discount coupon).
